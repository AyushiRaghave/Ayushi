# Portfolio go-to-market plan

**Release:** Portfolio launch package  
**Owner:** Ayushi Raghave  
**Status:** Launch package prepared; public launch is gated on production domain, deployment and live QA.

## 1. Launch objective

Turn the portfolio into a credible, easy-to-understand source of truth that creates qualified conversations—not simply page views.

Primary conversion: a relevant person contacts Ayushi with a specific opportunity, product challenge or collaboration request.

Secondary conversions:
- A product leader or hiring manager reviews a relevant case study and initiates a conversation.
- A founder or small business owner understands the scope of a digital product engagement and asks about fit.
- A peer or collaborator shares the work with someone relevant.

Do not optimise for vanity metrics such as raw impressions or time-on-page without connecting them to meaningful actions.

## 2. Positioning

### Core positioning

**Product management, strategy and UX for useful digital products.**

Ayushi brings customer needs, business goals and technology together to frame problems, shape product decisions and turn ideas into useful digital experiences.

### Proof points the portfolio can legitimately make

- Nearly three years of enterprise technology experience.
- product analysis and product concept work.
- A task-first AI workflow prototype (Promptless).
- Structured product thinking across problem framing, requirements, UX and technical feasibility.

Do not claim shipped products, client results, revenue impact, user growth, production AI capability or official affiliation where the portfolio does not provide evidence.

### Audience-specific message

| Audience | What they need to understand | Best route |
|---|---|---|
| Product leaders and hiring teams | How Ayushi frames problems, reasons through trade-offs and communicates product decisions | About → Work → relevant case study → Contact |
| Founders | Ability to clarify an idea and shape a useful product experience | Promptless / case studies → capabilities → Contact |
| Small businesses | What can be delivered, what is in scope and how to start a conversation | Capabilities → Contact |
| Peers and collaborators | Point of view, craft and work worth sharing | Selected work → LinkedIn |

Keep the portfolio's main identity anchored in product management and product strategy. Present websites, custom web applications and AI-powered tools as selected delivery capabilities—not as three unrelated professional identities.

## 3. Offer architecture

Use these three capability labels consistently:

1. **Websites & landing pages** — clear, conversion-aware digital experiences.
2. **Custom web applications** — focused web products shaped around a defined user need and workflow.
3. **AI-powered tools** — task-specific interfaces and connected AI experiences with clear human review.

Do not publish prices, turnaround times, guarantees or availability until they are deliberately defined. Before accepting a project, qualify the problem, intended users, scope, decision-maker, constraints and success measure.

## 4. Launch channels

### Owned
- Portfolio website: canonical source of truth.
- LinkedIn profile: professional distribution and credibility.
- Direct email: high-relevance, personalised outreach.

### Earned
- Relevant peer shares and recommendations.
- Thoughtful participation in product/design/AI communities where sharing is permitted.
- Case-study references from people who can on its own validate the work.

### Paid
- No paid acquisition for the initial launch. There is not yet a validated conversion baseline or offer economics to justify it.

## 5. Launch sequence

### Phase 0 — readiness gate
- Choose the production domain and hosting target.
- Configure HTTPS, canonical URLs, sitemap and robots.txt.
- Test every page and primary interaction on desktop and mobile.
- Confirm contact email and LinkedIn destination.
- Validate social preview cards on public URLs.
- Verify the privacy and data-handling language, especially Promptless webhook behaviour.
- Run accessibility and performance checks against the deployed site.

### Phase 1 — soft launch (first 3–5 days)
- Share privately with 5–8 trusted reviewers: ideally one product leader, one designer, one founder, one technical reviewer and two people unfamiliar with the work.
- Ask them to complete three tasks: explain what Ayushi does, find the strongest project, and identify how to contact her.
- Record confusion, broken interactions and missing evidence. Do not ask only whether they like the design.
- Fix comprehension and conversion blockers before broad promotion.

### Phase 2 — public launch (week 2)
- Publish one concise LinkedIn launch post with a clear point of view and a direct portfolio link.
- Publish one follow-up post focused on a specific product decision from a case study.
- Send 8–12 individual messages to highly relevant people, each with a specific reason for reaching out. Do not mass-message.
- Add the portfolio link to LinkedIn Featured and relevant professional profiles.

### Phase 3 — sustained distribution (weeks 3–4)
- Publish one useful case-study insight per week.
- Share one Promptless build/learning update that is transparent about prototype status.
- Make 5 thoughtful, relevant conversations per week; prioritise fit over volume.
- Review qualified conversations and project-page engagement weekly; refine the weakest audience path.

## 7. Measurement plan

Start with a privacy-conscious measurement approach. Do not add analytics scripts until the provider, retention settings, consent requirements and privacy notice have been reviewed. Basic hosting logs may be sufficient for the first soft launch.

| Funnel stage | Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Qualified visits by source | Whether relevant people are finding the site |
| Orientation | Contact-section reach; project-page visits | Whether visitors understand where to go |
| Consideration | Relevant project-page engagement; return visits | Whether the work earns further attention |
| Intent | Contact CTA clicks | Whether the offer and evidence create interest |
| Conversion | Qualified enquiries | Whether the site creates useful opportunities |
| Quality | Share of enquiries matching target audience and work | Whether positioning attracts the right people |

Suggested initial operating targets (not industry benchmarks or guarantees):
- 5–8 soft-launch reviewers complete the three comprehension tasks.
- At least 4 of 5 reviewers can accurately describe the core positioning after a brief visit.
- Zero critical broken links or blocked primary interactions at launch.
- 8–12 individually relevant outreach messages in the public-launch week.
- Review the first 10 substantive responses/enquiries before changing positioning or investing in paid distribution.

## 8. SEO, AEO and GEO distribution

- Keep one clear canonical URL per page after the domain is configured.
- Submit the sitemap to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools after deployment.
- Keep Person, WebSite, SoftwareApplication and CreativeWork structured data aligned with visible page content.
- Keep `llms.txt` factual, concise and consistent with the portfolio.
- Earn external references through useful work and relevant professional participation; do not buy low-quality backlinks or publish mass-generated SEO pages.
- Revisit case-study pages when product facts, API constraints or platform behaviour change.
- Do not promise rankings, AI citations or answer-engine inclusion.

## 9. Launch risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Portfolio appears to be an agency and a product-management profile at once | Keep the primary identity anchored in product management/strategy; make capabilities secondary |
| Concept work is mistaken for shipped work | Label  analysis, prototype and unvalidated concept status clearly |
| Promptless appears production-ready when the backend is not connected | Show connected/local mode truthfully; do not market live AI generation until tested end-to-end |
| Visitors do not know what action to take | One clear contact path and audience-specific route through the work |
| Search metadata points to a placeholder domain | Run the URL configuration script only with the final HTTPS URL |
| Performance or accessibility regression | Run real-browser, keyboard, screen-reader and Lighthouse checks after deployment |
| Analytics collects unnecessary personal data | Start without tracking scripts; add only after privacy review and a clear measurement need |

## 10. Decision rules after launch

- If visitors cannot describe the core positioning, simplify the hero and first-screen content before adding more sections.
- If people view projects but do not contact, inspect proof, CTA clarity and audience fit before changing colours or animations.
- If enquiries are broad but low-fit, narrow the capability descriptions and qualification path.
- If one project consistently attracts relevant conversations, give it more prominence and distribute its insight on its own.
- Do not treat low traffic in the first weeks as proof that the portfolio or offer has failed; assess distribution quality and message comprehension first.
